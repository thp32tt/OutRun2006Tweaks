No new original opcode/XREF, physical reflection-face producer, VS/PS/texture generation or optical route is asserted.
2. **Distinct CPU-cadence and XR-cadence domains.** The callback's static `lastCounter` samples `QueryPerformanceCounter` only when `Settings::VREnabled && Settings::VRNormalizeReflectionRate`; `frameScale60` derives from successive callbacks' `dt*60` at `src/hooks_graphics.cpp:1524-1570`. Neither `xrWaitFrame`, `xrEndFrame`, headset refresh nor SafeFrameId enters this cadence input. If the callback runs at 72 Hz while XR submits at 120 Hz, the estimator observes a ~13.89 ms *game callback* interval, not the 8.33 ms headset period. Conversely the callback may run during frames that do not yield a fresh XR projection. This is a source-confirmed **measurement-domain difference**, not proof of an observed stutter.
3. **Bounded face admission and slower re-lock.** `src/vr/game/reflection_frame_budget.hpp::AdaptiveFrameCadence::Observe:34-106` learns plausible non-late callback gaps, flags a gap above either 18 ms or (after four healthy samples) `referenceSeconds*1.20+0.0005`, and needs **24 consecutive** matches within 1.5% of a larger standard nominal period to re-lock. `::Advance:114-160` clamps reflection rate to [0,1], clears pending debt when rate is zero, uses `lateFrame ? 0.5 : clamp(dt*60,0,1)`, caps admitted faces at 2 on a VR-normalized late callback and 3 on a normal one, and bounds retained fractional debt to 1. Thus `ReflectionUpdateRate` is a frame-budget policy, not a per-GPU-pass time, per-eye pass count, or guaranteed 60/72/90/120-Hz refresh detector.
4. **Reproducible symbolic counterexample, NOT runtime evidence.** After the cadence reference has stabilized at 90 Hz, an exact 72-Hz callback interval (13.889 ms) exceeds `11.111*1.20+0.5 = 13.833 ms`; it is provisionally classed as late for 23 matching callbacks and on the 24th nominal match re-locks to 72 Hz. At `requestedRate=0.5`, a late callback adds 1.5 requested faces with a cap of 2, while a healthy 72-Hz callback adds ~2.5 with a cap of 3; fractional debt changes actual step counts. A 1.5%-outside-nominal jitter callback can clear the slower candidate. These are mathematical outcomes of checked-in code; a real 90→72 mode switch, 24 unbroken callbacks, visible reflection lag and the physical face draws were NOT observed.

1. At `vrhost/src/main_r23.cpp::R23AsyncPixelProbe::ScheduleProjection:1094-1147`, normalized sample X positions are `{.10,.30,.50,.70,.90}` and Y positions `{.20,.50,.80}`. The *same* `sx,sy` grid is sampled independently from source array slices eye=0 and eye=1 into consecutive staging-row pixels, with no feature/pose disparity compensation. `D3D11CalcSubresource(0,eye,1)` distinguishes arrays; the fact of two eye slices does not itself indicate an extra native lens draw.
2. `ConsumeProjection:865-998` decodes each eye's RGB at the *same index* and computes `pairAbsMean` and `pairAbsMax` from `abs(decoded[0][i][c]-decoded[1][i][c])` (specifically :927-948). Neither binocular centroid matching nor per-eye camera projection is used in the pair metric. Raw RGBA hashes are separate and have the 01548 alpha caveat.
3. `R23RenderProjection:1627-1699` explicitly renders `eyes[0]` to `rtvs[image][0]` and `eyes[1]` to `rtvs[image][1]`, then sets `pv[eye].pose=views[eye].pose`, `pv[eye].fov=views[eye].fov`, and `pv[eye].subImage.imageArrayIndex=eye`. Thus physically intended L/R input rays and submitted per-eye views may differ. Nothing in the pixel-grid comparison inverse-projects the measured screen positions through those eye views.
4. Constructive **synthetic array counterexample, NOT GPU/HMD observation**: all 15 samples in each eye decode to RGB (0,0,0) and identical alpha except one red-only RGB=(1,0,0) sample per eye. Put eye L's one bright point at normalized (.30,.50) and eye R's at (.50,.50). This represents ONE source feature at different L/R projected screen coordinates, not two same-eye discs. Both have `finite=15`; `pairAbsMax=1` and `pairAbsMean=2/45=0.044444...` because two corresponding red-index comparisons differ, with no duplicate feature in either eye. The physical reasonableness of that particular disparity is NOT claimed: it is a mathematical counterexample to the **universal inference** `nonzero pairAbs => duplicate or broken stereo`.
5. Constructive opposite control: put the same single bright point at (.30,.50) in both eye arrays. `pairAbsMean=pairAbsMax=0` and same raw hash given identical pixels/alpha. This can represent an inappropriate screen/head-locked mono flare, OR an optically valid approximately zero-disparity distant light, OR an intentionally fused centre-screen C993 image. Equality alone cannot select among them. A real pose/feature-bearing experiment is necessary.
6. `src/hooks_graphics.cpp::DrawObjectAlphaCentre:945-1059` explicitly retains C993 objects 0x570002/006/00F/010 and its narrow centre-eye fusion semantics, while `DrawObjectAlphaProjected:797-905` retains BOTH 0x570008 source ordinals. These classes should not be assumed to share one intended disparity. `R30TryScreenSpaceFovDraw:5778+` warns that an inline source call is a bounded hint and the deferred 4052C0 opaque queue cannot be assigned an exact 570008 PMT identity from its window.
7. This review only invalidates use of *same-coordinate sampled RGB difference* as a universal binocular stereo/duplicate verdict. It does NOT say the grid cannot measure image differences, does NOT prove a real flare has nonzero disparity, and does NOT establish whether the user's visible centre double image originates in game, R30/R26, or host/OpenXR. The 01547 sparse ROI blind spots and 01548 RGBA-vs-RGB caveats remain independent.
